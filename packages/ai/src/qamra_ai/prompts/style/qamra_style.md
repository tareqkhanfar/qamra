# Qamra house illustration style

Injected into every image prompt: pages, cover, background plates, and character and companion sheets.
The page prompt assembles the sections in this order: Style → Setting → scene → characters → outfit →
Composition → Safety → Negative (Addendum 3 §3).

To tune the look, edit this file, not the code. Keep each section short and visual; image models follow
concrete instructions best. Change the version line on every edit so books record which style drew them.

version: 4

## Style
A premium children's picture-book illustration. The medium and rendering come from the art-style line below
(watercolor, 3D animated film or bright 2D cartoon); everything else here is shared by every style.
The palette is warm moonlit-and-golden: deep indigo and night blue, moon gold and honey, and creamy whites,
with gentle sage-green and coral accents. Light is warm with a golden glow, and every scene has depth:
foreground, midground and background. Shapes are clean and readable, and backgrounds uncluttered.
Characters are friendly and rounded, with expressive, happy faces and warm eyes. Children have slightly
large heads and short limbs but real faces, never chibi or doll-like. Not photorealistic, not anime.

## Setting
Unless the scene says otherwise, the world is Palestinian and Levantine village and city life:
- warm limestone houses with arched windows and flat roofs;
- stone terraces and garden walls, olive groves with silvery leaves;
- prickly pear cactus, grapevines over a pergola, lemon trees in clay pots;
- old souq arches, patterned cement-tile floors;
- family living rooms with floor cushions and embroidered pillows.

Skies are wide and soft. Avoid Tuscan and European cues: no rows of cypress trees, no Italian villas, no
red-roofed European towns, no snowy pine forests.

## People
Everyone wears modest, everyday clothing. The hero wears a hijab only when this prompt says the parent chose
it. Other girls and women appear with and without head coverings, in natural variety. Grandparents and
families are warm, dignified and respectful: no caricature, no stereotypes. Children look their real age.

## Composition
Stage it like a picture book: one clear focal action, the hero large and easy to read, and an eye-level or
slightly low camera. Keep faces and important details out of the outer 6% of the image, because print trims
it. Always leave the text area named below calm and simple (soft sky, a plain wall or a gently blurred
background) with no faces, hands or key objects in it.

## Safety
Everything is gentle and safe for ages 3–7: nothing scary, violent or dangerous. A child is never alone near
water, fire, a road, a cliff or a power outlet; an adult stays close in such places. Nobody is hurt, crying
in fear, or in danger. No medicine, no weapons, and no risky behavior shown as fun.

## Negative
- No text of any kind: no letters, words, numbers, signs with writing, book titles, labels, speech bubbles,
  logos, brand names or watermarks. All text is added later by the book designer.
- No page numbers, captions, signatures, stamps or marks in the corners: the printed book adds its own page numbers.
- Books, boxes, shelves and walls stay plain: book spines and covers are blocks of color, with no titles;
  no alphabet posters, charts, calendars, name labels or price tags.
- No photorealism, no neon colors.
- No extra or missing fingers, no extra limbs, no distorted, melted or duplicated faces.
- No existing cartoon, film or game characters.
